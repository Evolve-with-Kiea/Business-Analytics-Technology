## 7. Business Problem Framing

### Why It Matters

An analysis is more useful when the question is clearly defined.

A broad concern such as "sales are declining" does not automatically explain what should be measured or investigated.

### Problem-Framing Framework

**Step 1: Identify the concern.**

What issue has been reported?

**Step 2: Define the business objective.**

What decision or outcome matters?

**Step 3: Convert the concern into an answerable question.**

What specific information is needed?

**Step 4: Identify the relevant measures.**

Which KPIs or variables help answer the question?

**Step 5: Define the scope.**

What time period, business segment, or dataset will be examined?

### Example

**Business Concern:** Customer payments appear to be arriving later.

**Business Question:** Has the percentage of receivables more than 60 days overdue increased during the past six months?

**Relevant Measures:**

- Total outstanding accounts receivable
- Percentage of receivables more than 60 days overdue
- Aging category balances
- Collection trends

**Potential Decision:** Determine whether additional collections review or process changes are warranted.

## 8. Data Quality & Analytical Reliability

### Objective

Understand whether information is suitable for the intended analysis before drawing conclusions.

### Data Quality Dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Does the data correctly represent the underlying activity? |
| Completeness | Are required records or values missing? |
| Consistency | Are definitions and formats used uniformly? |
| Timeliness | Is the information current enough for the decision? |
| Validity | Do values follow expected rules and formats? |
| Uniqueness | Are duplicate records present where they should not be? |
| Relevance | Does the dataset answer the business question? |

### Data Review Checklist

- [ ] Identify the source.
- [ ] Understand what each field represents.
- [ ] Check for missing information.
- [ ] Review duplicate records.
- [ ] Verify dates and numerical formats.
- [ ] Investigate unusual values.
- [ ] Confirm metric definitions.
- [ ] Document corrections.
- [ ] Record unresolved limitations.

**Analytical principle:** A technically correct calculation can still produce a misleading conclusion when the underlying data is incomplete, inaccurate, or inappropriate.

## 10. Critical Thinking & Analytical Judgment

### Objective

Develop the ability to question information, consider alternative explanations, and avoid unsupported conclusions.

### Questions to Ask

- Is the data reliable?
- Is the comparison fair?
- Are the time periods consistent?
- Could seasonality explain the change?
- Are there missing variables?
- Could an unusual event affect the results?
- Is the observed relationship causal or merely correlated?
- What assumptions are being made?
- What additional evidence would strengthen the conclusion?
- What would change my interpretation?

### Example

**Observation:** Revenue increased by 15%.

**Possible Incorrect Conclusion:** The business is performing better in every respect.

**Additional Questions:**

- Did expenses increase faster than revenue?
- Did gross margin decline?
- Were discounts responsible for higher sales?
- Did the business generate additional cash?
- Is the increase seasonal?

**Analytical Lesson:** A single positive metric does not necessarily indicate stronger overall performance.

## 11. Data Visualization & Analytical Storytelling

### Objective

Present information in a way that helps the intended audience understand performance, trends, comparisons, and potential actions.

### Chart Selection Guide

| Analytical Purpose | Common Visualization |
|---|---|
| Show trends over time | Line chart |
| Compare categories | Bar or column chart |
| Show proportions | Stacked bar chart or another suitable composition view |
| Show relationships | Scatter plot |
| Show distribution | Histogram |
| Display key measures | KPI cards |
| Present detailed figures | Table or matrix |

### Analytical Storytelling Structure

**1. Business Question**

What are we trying to understand?

**2. Evidence**

What information was examined?

**3. Findings**

What does the analysis show?

**4. Interpretation**

Why might the findings matter?

**5. Recommendation**

What action could be considered?

**6. Limitations**

What remains uncertain?

### Communication Standard

Reports and dashboards should be understandable to business stakeholders who may not have technical backgrounds.

Technical details should remain available for verification, but the main message should be clear and actionable.

## 12. Decision-Making & Recommendation Development

### Objective

Move beyond describing data toward evaluating what the information means for a business decision.

### Recommendation Framework

| Element | Guiding Question |
|---|---|
| Finding | What did the analysis identify? |
| Business Impact | Why does the finding matter? |
| Supporting Evidence | Which results support the conclusion? |
| Proposed Action | What should be considered? |
| Risks | What could go wrong? |
| Limitations | What remains unknown? |
| Success Measure | How would improvement be evaluated? |

### Example

**Finding:** A sample aging report shows an increasing share of receivables older than 60 days.

**Interpretation:** The business may face increased collections risk and slower cash conversion.

**Potential Recommendation:** Review overdue account patterns, investigate payment delays, and evaluate existing follow-up procedures.

**Success Measures:** Monitor aging balances, collection rates, and DSO over subsequent periods.

**Important:** A recommendation should be proportional to the quality and completeness of the evidence.

## 14. Practical Business Analysis Workflow

I will use the following framework when completing analytical exercises and future portfolio projects.

### Step 1 — Define

Identify the business question and intended decision.

### Step 2 — Understand

Review the business context, stakeholders, and available information.

### Step 3 — Prepare

Inspect, clean, validate, and organize the dataset.

### Step 4 — Analyze

Perform relevant calculations, comparisons, and investigations.

### Step 5 — Interpret

Explain the findings in business terms.

### Step 6 — Communicate

Present results through summaries, tables, charts, or dashboards.

### Step 7 — Recommend

Identify reasonable actions or further questions.

### Step 8 — Document

Record methods, assumptions, limitations, and supporting evidence.

### Step 9 — Review & Improve

Evaluate feedback, correct problems, and refine the analysis.
