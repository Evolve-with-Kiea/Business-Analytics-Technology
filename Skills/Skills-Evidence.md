## 1. Skills Assessment Framework

### Technical & Analytical Skill Levels

| Level | Definition | Supporting Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Planned | I intend to learn the skill but have not started structured study or practice. | Learning plan or development objective |
| Learning | I am studying the skill and developing an understanding of foundational concepts. | Lesson notes, course activity, or documented study |
| Practicing | I can complete guided exercises and am working toward independent application. | Exercises, screenshots, calculations, or practice files |
| Applied | I have independently used the skill to complete a meaningful task or project. | Completed project, analysis, report, or documented work |
| Demonstrated | I can independently apply the skill, explain my methods, and communicate the results. | Reviewed project, multiple work samples, or substantial professional evidence |

### Professional Experience Classification

Established professional competencies are evaluated separately from technical learning levels.

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Professional Experience | Skill developed through prior or current employment |
| Documented Professional Experience | Professional capability supported by employment history, resume descriptions, or other appropriate records |
| Portfolio Demonstrated | Skill supported by a shareable project, work sample, or independently developed case study |
| Continuing Development | Existing professional knowledge being strengthened through additional study or practice |

**Important distinction:** Lack of a public portfolio file does not mean a professional skill has never been used. However, professional experience and portfolio evidence should be represented separately.

## 5. Evidence Types & Verification Standards

Not all evidence demonstrates the same level of capability.

| Evidence Type | What It Can Demonstrate | Important Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Study Notes | Understanding of concepts | Does not establish independent application |
| Course Participation | Exposure to structured instruction | Participation alone does not prove proficiency |
| Guided Exercise | Ability to follow and understand a process | May rely on instructional assistance |
| Independent Exercise | Ability to apply a concept without step-by-step guidance | May cover a limited task |
| Completed Project | Ability to apply multiple skills to a business problem | Quality and independence still require review |
| Dashboard or Report | Ability to organize and communicate results | Visual presentation alone does not establish analytical accuracy |
| Professional Experience | Real-world application in a business setting | Details may not be publicly shareable |
| Certification | Completion of a defined credential requirement | Does not automatically establish advanced practical proficiency |
| Academic Coursework | Formal learning and assessed work | Specific capabilities depend on assignments completed |
| Project Reflection | Understanding of decisions, challenges, and limitations | Supports but does not replace practical evidence |

### Evidence Strength

**Foundational Evidence:** Notes, concept explanations, and introductory exercises.

**Practice Evidence:** Guided activities, calculations, queries, and documented exercises.

**Application Evidence:** Independent analysis, dashboards, reports, scripts, and case studies.

**Professional Evidence:** Relevant employment experience and appropriately documented business responsibilities.

## 10. Project Evidence Quality Checklist

Before linking a project as evidence:

### Ownership & Integrity

- [ ] The work accurately represents my contribution.
- [ ] Tutorials, templates, and external materials are acknowledged.
- [ ] Guided work is identified as guided.
- [ ] AI assistance is disclosed when relevant to understanding authorship.
- [ ] No confidential or restricted information is included.

### Analytical Quality

- [ ] The project has a clearly defined purpose.
- [ ] The data source is identified.
- [ ] Dataset permissions or licenses have been reviewed.
- [ ] Calculations and methods are documented.
- [ ] Outputs have been checked.
- [ ] Findings are explained.
- [ ] Assumptions and limitations are identified.

### Documentation Quality

- [ ] The README explains the project.
- [ ] Files are organized logically.
- [ ] Screenshots are readable where used.
- [ ] Links work correctly.
- [ ] The skills demonstrated are explicitly identified.
- [ ] The completion status is accurate.
