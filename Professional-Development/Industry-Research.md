# Industry Research

## Career Intelligence, Labor Market Analysis & Professional Development

### Purpose

This document serves as a structured research framework for evaluating career opportunities, employer expectations, industry applications, technical skill requirements, professional certifications, and emerging trends across Business Analytics, Data Analytics, Business Intelligence, Financial Analysis, and related disciplines.

With more than 20 years of experience in accounting, financial operations, bookkeeping, reconciliations, and enterprise business systems, I am exploring opportunities to combine my established business expertise with developing analytical and technical capabilities.

As I pursue my Associate of Applied Science in Business Analytics at Wake Technical Community College, with anticipated completion in Summer 2027, this research supports informed decisions about professional specialization, continuing education, technical training, portfolio development, and long-term career advancement.

**Primary objective:** Identify career paths that align with my existing experience, developing skills, professional interests, and the demonstrated requirements of employers.

This is a living research document. Trackers below are templates for future research and do not represent completed employer or labor-market findings.

---

## 1. Career Research Objectives

My research focuses on six areas:

1. **Career Alignment:** Identify roles that combine business knowledge, financial expertise, analytical thinking, and technology.
2. **Employer Expectations:** Understand which qualifications, tools, and competencies appear in relevant job postings.
3. **Industry Opportunities:** Evaluate how organizations use analytics across different business environments.
4. **Skill Development:** Identify gaps between my current capabilities and employer requirements.
5. **Professional Growth:** Explore certifications, educational pathways, networking opportunities, and career progression.
6. **Market Awareness:** Monitor changes in analytics responsibilities, reporting technologies, automation, and AI-assisted workflows.

### Questions Guiding My Research

- Which roles provide the strongest connection between my accounting experience and business analytics?
- Which technical skills appear most frequently in relevant job postings?
- Which employers emphasize practical experience versus specific degrees or certifications?
- How do job responsibilities differ between positions with similar titles?
- Which industries offer opportunities to apply financial and operational knowledge?
- What portfolio projects would demonstrate relevant capabilities?
- Which career paths offer opportunities for advancement into more specialized analytical work?
- How are artificial intelligence and automation changing analytical responsibilities?
- Which skills should I prioritize during my associate degree and subsequent bachelor's degree studies?

## 3. Research Methodology

### Step 1: Select a Career Role

Choose a position from [Roles to Explore](../Career-Interests/Roles-to-Explore.md).

Define the role's primary responsibilities and identify alternative job titles that may describe similar work.

### Step 2: Review Current Job Postings

Collect a manageable sample of relevant job advertisements.

When practical, compare approximately 5–10 postings for a role before drawing preliminary conclusions.

Record:

- Job title
- Employer and industry
- Location and work arrangement
- Responsibilities
- Required qualifications
- Preferred qualifications
- Technical tools
- Certifications
- Experience expectations
- Salary information, when disclosed
- Date reviewed
- Original source link

Avoid assuming that requirements from one employer represent the entire market.

### Step 3: Consult Occupational Research Sources

Use authoritative sources to understand occupations, educational expectations, employment trends, and related skills.

**Core research sources:**

- [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/)
- [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/)
- [CareerOneStop](https://www.careeronestop.org/)
- [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/oes/)

**Additional research sources:**

- Employer career websites
- LinkedIn job postings
- Indeed job listings
- Professional association publications
- Official technology documentation
- University and college program descriptions
- Industry research reports

Research findings should include dates and source references, particularly when describing salaries, hiring trends, or skill demand.

### Step 4: Identify Patterns

Compare multiple sources to determine:

- Frequently requested technical skills
- Common business knowledge requirements
- Repeated software platforms
- Typical educational qualifications
- Experience expectations
- Industry-specific requirements
- Opportunities for transferable skills

Distinguish recurring patterns from isolated employer preferences.

### Step 5: Evaluate My Current Readiness

Compare identified requirements with my documented capabilities in [Technical Skills](../Skills/Technical-Skills.md) and [Skills Evidence](../Skills/Skills-Evidence.md).

### Step 6: Translate Findings Into Action

Use research results to prioritize:

- Academic coursework
- Independent learning
- Technical exercises
- Portfolio projects
- Certification research
- Professional networking
- Resume development
- Job applications

## 15. Research Standards & Documentation

To maintain a credible and useful research record, I will:

- Record research dates and original sources.
- Distinguish verified information from assumptions.
- Avoid generalizing from a single job posting.
- Compare required qualifications with preferred qualifications.
- Consider geographic and industry differences.
- Document salary information only when supported by a source.
- Distinguish employer requirements from personal learning priorities.
- Update findings as technology and hiring expectations change.
- Avoid presenting proposed research as completed findings.
- Protect private information shared during professional conversations.
- Use research findings to guide realistic learning and career decisions.
